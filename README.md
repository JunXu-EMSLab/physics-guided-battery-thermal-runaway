# Physics-guided learning predicts battery thermal runaway from parsimonious features

**Authors:** Sihui Chen, Ryan Wang, Mengyuan Zhou, Shuguo Sun, and Jun Xu

**Corresponding author:** Jun Xu ([junxu@psu.edu](mailto:junxu@psu.edu))

**Version date:** 09/09/2026

> Preprint: this version has been reviewed and is under revision based on anonymous reviewers' feedback.

## Abstract

Thermal-runaway heat release determines the thermal-management and propagation-resistance requirements of battery systems, yet its calorimetric characterization is destructive, hazardous, and too time-consuming to cover the expanding diversity of cell designs and operating states. Existing predictive approaches often depend on specialized calorimetry, post-failure measurements, or high-dimensional inputs. Here, we develop a physics-guided feature-engineering framework to estimate capacity-normalized corrected heat release from routinely accessible cell data. An energy-balance description guides the construction of compact descriptors representing stored electrochemical energy and cell geometry. The framework is evaluated retrospectively using 338 fractional thermal-runaway calorimetry tests on 18650 and 21700 cylindrical cells. A composite descriptor formed from open-circuit voltage, state of charge, and capacity achieves a median mean absolute percentage error of 10.94% for 21700 cells, while two constructed descriptors achieve 13.84% for 18650 cells and 13.80% for the pooled dataset. These low-dimensional representations retain much of the predictive accuracy of a 16-variable baseline while requiring only routine pretest measurements. The results establish a compact surrogate for screening normalized heat-release behavior within the represented experimental domain. The methodology highlights the importance of physical laws in feature construction and selection and provides a basis for prioritizing experiments where calorimetry can deliver the greatest additional information.
